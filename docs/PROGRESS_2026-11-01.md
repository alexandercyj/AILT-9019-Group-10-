## The data-sufficiency round, and what it refused

The post-midterm note asked for two things: fix the data-sufficiency problem by looking at
the Palace Museum and the British Museum, reading their robots files, filtering records and
expanding the database; and improve the integration scheme while doing it.

The second half was done. The first half was answered, but not as asked: **both museums were
refused**, and the refusal is recorded rather than worked around. Stating that plainly
matters more than the count of records it added.

| Museum | robots.txt | Licence for records | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| British Museum | 200, `Content-Signal: search=yes, ai-train=no, use=reference`, training crawlers disallowed | none published | **refused** — also 403 via Cloudflare challenge to non-browser clients |
| Palace Museum 故宫 | 404 with a 70-byte meta refresh, no file published | none published, no API | **refused** — nothing to read, and a missing file is not a grant |
| Art Institute of Chicago | 403 Cloudflare challenge, no verdict reachable | CC0 | deferred, as the proposal's declared fallback |
| Wikidata | Action API allowed; `/sparql` disallowed | CC0 | reference only — an aggregator record has no credit line |
| Cleveland Museum of Art | 200, only Amazonbot disallowed | CC0, filterable at fetch time | **admitted** — 10 records frozen |

What the round produced instead:

1. **A third corpus, from a second museum.** `data/frozen_corpus_cleveland.json`, 10 records,
   filtered to `share_license_status == "CC0"` at fetch time. Its value is not the count: its
   dates are phrased by Cleveland, not by us (`c. 1050`, `1100s`, `400–700`,
   `1800s, before 1896`). A hedge rule that only survives our own wording is not a rule.
2. **A source register that is enforced.** `data/sources.json` plus `labeldesk/sources.py`.
   `pipeline.draft()` asks whether the corpus's source permits `ai_input` *before* the model
   step, so an unregistered corpus or a refused source cannot reach the network through any
   entry point. Exit code 4, reason `source_policy`, offline draft printed.
3. **A way to check the register later.** `tools/check_sources.py` re-fetches every
   `robots.txt`, parses it rather than only hashing it, and reports `status_changed`,
   `body_changed`, `agent_blocked`, `ai_input_contradicted`. Seven of seven sources currently
   match.
4. **A route for the refused records.** `tools/import_records.py` takes a CSV of the five
   fields plus the licence the importer asserts and the basis on which they hold the records.
   `--ai-input` is off unless asked for: a licence to read records is not a licence to send
   them to a model. A misaligned row is refused, not imported — an unquoted comma in a credit
   line is the failure mode this tool must not produce.
5. **An evaluation that is not tied to the golden set.** `eval/cross_corpus.py` runs all 40
   records across all three corpora with no per-record expectations: 0 check failures, 35 of
   37 rewrites accepted, the two discards being `drops_field_content` — the coverage rule
   doing its job on data the golden set does not cover.

The reasoning source by source is `docs/DATA_SOURCES.md`.

  repository.
- **The 60-second recording.** Exists for the midterm (`docs/demo.mp4`); the model layer is
  not in it. If the final submission wants the model path on camera, it needs re-recording.
  `docs/DEMO_SCRIPT.md` has the shot list; the Cleveland corpus gives it a second beat.
- **The final report.** Not written. The material for it is `eval/reports/`,
  `docs/MODEL_LAYER_DECISION.md`, `docs/DATA_SOURCES.md` and this page.
- **Acceptance rate above 85%.** The two discarded rewrites are a coverage failure, not a
  factual one. Raising the rate means teaching the model to keep the full medium value;
  lowering the bar would mean weakening `validate_coverage`, which the decision note rules
  out. On the Cleveland corpus the rate is 10/10, which suggests the failure is specific to
  two long Met medium strings rather than general.
- **A Chinese-language label.** The corpus values are English because the source records are
  English; a Chinese label would need a translation step that the five-field contract cannot
  currently cite. Not in the proposal, but worth naming before someone assumes it is.
